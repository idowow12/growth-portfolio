# Example / template: long-form VSL outline

> **Example / template.** A structure I use for long-form video sales letters, shown with a hypothetical dog calming chew. It is not a script from a past client.

## Structure

| # | Section | Job | Example (hypothetical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Stop the scroll with the pain or a bold promise | "If your dog panics every time you leave, this is for you." |
| 2 | **Lead / story** | Make the viewer feel understood | The owner who dreads leaving the house |
| 3 | **Problem agitation** | Show the cost of doing nothing | Stress for the dog, guilt for the owner, damage at home |
| 4 | **Why old solutions fail** | Clear the ground of what they already tried | Training alone, sprays, generic treats |
| 5 | **New mechanism** | Introduce why this is different | The ingredient story and how it supports calm |
| 6 | **Product reveal** | Name it and show it | The chew, what is in it, how it is used |
| 7 | **Proof** | Make the claim believable | Customer stories, reviews, a demo |
| 8 | **Objection handling** | Answer doubts before they are asked | Safety, taste, how long it takes |
| 9 | **Offer stack** | Make the deal feel obvious | Bundle, bonus, guarantee |
| 10 | **Risk reversal** | Remove the fear of buying | Money-back guarantee |
| 11 | **Urgency** | Give a reason to act now | A real, honest reason only |
| 12 | **CTA and recap** | Say what to do | Button, summary of the promise |

## Writing notes
- Write the first 30 seconds as its own ad, since many viewers never pass them.
- Use customer language from reviews and comments, not brand language.
- Cut every line that does not move the viewer to the next section.
- Test two or three different hooks on the same body.
