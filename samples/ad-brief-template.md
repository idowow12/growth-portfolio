# Example / template: one-page ad brief

> **Example / template.** This is a hypothetical brief for an imaginary product, written to show my format. It is not past client work and the product, audience and numbers are invented for illustration.

## Brief

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Product** | "PawCalm", a hypothetical calming chew for anxious dogs |
| **Ad ID** | PC-ANX-H03-UGC-9x16 |
| **Goal** | Purchase at or below target CAC |
| **Audience** | Dog owners whose dog struggles when left alone |
| **Awareness level** | Problem-aware: knows the problem, does not know this solution |
| **Angle** | "Come home to a calm house" |
| **Core belief to shift** | "My dog's anxiety is just something I have to live with" |
| **Hook (first 3 seconds)** | "My neighbor left a note about my dog barking, so I tried this." |
| **Proof** | Reviews, the ingredient story, a simple before/after day-in-the-life |
| **Offer** | Starter bundle with a guarantee |
| **CTA** | "Try the starter bundle" |
| **Format** | UGC, 9:16, 25-35 seconds, filmed at home |
| **Must show** | Dog, product in hand, the moment of leaving the house |
| **Must avoid** | Medical claims, "cures", before/after claims about health |
| **Hypothesis** | Social-pressure hooks beat product-first hooks with problem-aware buyers |

## Structure
1. **Hook (0-3s):** the pain or social moment.
2. **Problem (3-10s):** show what it looks like at home.
3. **Discovery (10-18s):** how the creator found the product, with what they were skeptical about.
4. **Proof (18-27s):** what changed, in plain words.
5. **CTA (27-35s):** the offer and next step.

## Variants to request
- 3 alternative hooks, same body (use the [hook bank](hook-bank.md))
- 1 static version from the strongest line
