Own D2C gadget brand · phone-commerce model · Oct 2026

30 products, one ad set each, 8.35% CTR in week one

My own Hebrew-language store selling 30 problem-solving gadgets, closed by phone and SMS. The goal of launch week: find which products pull, fast, on €50 a day.

What I did

  • Wrote and produced one Hebrew voice-over video and one static for every product (60 ads), each built on a single pain: "a cat that won't drink", "the car that won't connect", "the door you can't see through".
  • Put each product in its own ad set under one CBO budget, so spend flows to the products people actually want and the losers starve on their own.
  • Built a branded landing page per product, never supplier photos, and sent every lead straight to the dialer.
What it proved: creative-led product discovery works at small budgets. A €4 lead closed by phone is profitable; a €4 click on a cold product page is not.

US B2B · my agency, Ridgeline · booked calls

30 booked sales calls at a €115 CPM, and the ad Meta starved

Selling a done-for-you ad service to US business owners: the most expensive audience on Meta. Budget: €50 a day. The only thing that matters is booked calls.

What I did

  • Wrote 30 ads on different angles (cost, time, "I already tried Facebook", proof) and sent them to a one-step booking page.
  • Tracked every booking back to the ad through UTM per ad, instead of trusting Meta's own attribution.
  • Found that the best ad booked calls at €6.53 each while Meta gave it €19 in total, and poured €145 into an ad booking at €73. The ad set had no conversion signal, so Meta was optimizing for cheap clicks.
Lesson I now apply everywhere: "let Meta decide" only works once Meta has a signal for what a good outcome is. Until then, read cost per real outcome per ad yourself, and protect the winners.

Offer testing · own D2C · 4 offers, one product

Four offers tested, all four killed for under €500

Not every test wins, and the skill is losing cheaply. I ran the same product with four different offers (quantity bundle, free gift, add-on kit, launch discount), each on its own landing page.

What I did

  • Read ROAS per offer, then stopped all four (the best was 0.93 ROAS).
  • Moved the budget to new products instead of "optimizing" a product the market had already answered on.
The point: a kill rule written before the test is worth more than any optimization after it.

Ads I made

A sample of the 300+ ads I designed and launched this year. Every one was written to a single angle and judged on cost per real outcome.

How I work

The weekly system behind the numbers. Volume of genuinely different concepts, tight briefs, and kill rules written before the money is spent.

01

Mine the customer's words

Reviews, comments, support tickets, sales-call transcripts. I pull the exact phrases people use for the pain and the outcome, and every angle starts from one of them.

02

Angles before ads

One angle = one belief to change. Each angle gets its own hook bank (5-10 openings) split by awareness level: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware.

03

Brief at volume

15+ briefs a week, each one page: angle, hook, the first 2 seconds shot by shot, proof moment, CTA, and what "winning" means for that ad.

04

Produce three ways

UGC creators recruited by ads and paid $18-20 per video; statics designed in-house; AI video and images (Higgsfield, Kling, Nano Banana) for fast variations.

05

Launch for a clean read

One CBO, broad audience, automatic placements, enough budget per ad to reach a decision. No 12 ad sets at €8 each that never leave learning.

06

Kill, scale, iterate

Kill on the rule, not on mood. Scale the top 5-10%. Then turn each winner into new hooks, formats and creators, because a new hook is not a new concept.

Judge on the outcome you are paid for

Clicks and CTR tell you whether people stopped. Cost per purchase, booked call or qualified lead tells you whether to keep paying.

From my own account: the ad with the best cost per booked call (€6.53) got €19 of spend; Meta preferred one at €73.
Don't split a small budget

At €50/day, every extra ad set is a slice too thin to learn. One CBO with many creatives beats five ad sets every time.

From my own account: 30 ads on €50/day, 3 ads took 84% of spend and 22 got under 30 impressions a week. Fewer, better ads per set.
Know the arithmetic before calling a bug

Budget ÷ CPM → impressions × CTR → clicks × page rate → leads. If the math says one lead a day, two empty days is noise.

€50/day at a €108 CPM ≈ 1 lead/day. CTR is the only lever at that budget.
Creative is the targeting

Broad audiences, automatic placements. The hook decides who stops. Narrow targeting mostly raises CPM.

Advantage+ audiences override age and interest settings anyway; the ad is what qualifies.
